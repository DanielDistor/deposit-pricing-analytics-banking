# Fed Rate Cycle Analysis: 2022–2024

## The 2022–2023 Hiking Cycle

### Timeline

The fastest rate-hiking cycle in 40 years:

| Date | Rate Change | New Target Range |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| March 16, 2022 | +25 bps | 0.25–0.50% |
| May 4, 2022 | +50 bps | 0.75–1.00% |
| June 15, 2022 | +75 bps | 1.50–1.75% |
| July 27, 2022 | +75 bps | 2.25–2.50% |
| September 21, 2022 | +75 bps | 3.00–3.25% |
| November 2, 2022 | +75 bps | 3.75–4.00% |
| December 14, 2022 | +50 bps | 4.25–4.50% |
| February 1, 2023 | +25 bps | 4.50–4.75% |
| March 22, 2023 | +25 bps | 4.75–5.00% |
| May 3, 2023 | +25 bps | 5.00–5.25% |
| July 26, 2023 | +25 bps | 5.25–5.50% |

**Total: 11 hikes, +525 basis points over 16 months**

Context: The Fed started hiking because inflation hit 9.1% YoY in June 2022 — the highest since 1981 — driven by pandemic-era supply chain disruptions, fiscal stimulus, and energy price spikes following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

### Bank Responses to Hikes

**Online Banks — Tracked the Fed**

| Bank | Pre-Hike APY (Feb 2022) | Peak APY (2023) | Pass-Through |
|------|------------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Marcus (Goldman) | ~0.50% | ~4.50% | ~76% |
| Ally Bank | ~0.50% | ~4.35% | ~73% |
| SoFi | ~0.20% | ~4.50–4.60% | ~80%+ |
| Discover | ~0.45% | ~4.35% | ~74% |

Online banks raised rates quickly because they must compete for deposits on rate — they have no branch convenience to fall back on. Rate is their product.

**Traditional Banks — Did Not Track the Fed**

| Bank | Pre-Hike APY (Feb 2022) | Peak APY (2023) | Pass-Through |
|------|------------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Chase | ~0.01% | ~0.01% | <1% |
| Bank of America | ~0.01% | ~0.01–0.04% | <1% |
| Wells Fargo | ~0.01% | ~0.01% | <1% |
| Citibank | ~0.01–0.04% | ~0.04% | <1% |

Traditional banks barely moved because their deposit bases are "sticky." Most customers don't switch banks regardless of rate — they value the branch network, relationship history, and inertia. Banks exploit this to maintain wide spreads during hiking cycles.

### Why the Divergence?

**Online bank incentive**: No branches = no relationship stickiness = must offer top rates to attract/retain deposits. When the Fed raises rates, online banks quickly follow to stay competitive.

**Traditional bank incentive**: Massive sticky deposit base (Chase has ~$1T+ in deposits). Even if 5% of customers leave, that's acceptable compared to the profit from raising rates on loans while keeping deposit costs near zero.

**The math**: During 2022–2023, Chase's net interest margin expanded significantly because loan rates rose with the Fed while deposit costs barely moved. This is one mechanism by which banks profit from rising rate environments.

## The 2024 Cutting Cycle

### Timeline

| Date | Rate Change | New Target Range |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| September 18, 2024 | −50 bps | 4.75–5.00% |
| November 7, 2024 | −25 bps | 4.50–4.75% |
| December 18, 2024 | −25 bps | 4.25–4.50% |

**Total: 3 cuts, −100 basis points in 2024**

The Fed cut rates as inflation retreated toward 2% and labor market conditions softened. The September 2024 cut was the first since March 2020 (COVID emergency cuts).

### Bank Responses to Cuts

Online banks began lowering their savings APYs within days of each Fed announcement — faster than they raised them, in some cases. By early 2025:
- Marcus: ~4.10% (down from ~4.50% peak)
- Ally: ~4.00% (down from ~4.35% peak)
- SoFi: ~4.30% (for members with direct deposit)

Traditional banks: minimal change — savings rates at Chase and BofA remained near 0.01%. There's little room to "cut" from 0.01%.

---

## Pass-Through Asymmetry

An important asymmetry: online banks are quick to raise AND quick to lower rates. Traditional banks are slow in both directions — but because they started near zero, their "cuts" are invisible.

This means:
- **During hikes**: Online banks benefit savers; traditional banks benefit shareholders
- **During cuts**: Online banks lower rates (savers lose some yield); traditional banks already at floor

## Implications for Savers

### Dollar Impact (Per $100,000)

At the 2023 peak (Fed rate: 5.25–5.50%):

| Bank | APY | Annual Interest on $100K |
|------|-----|--------------------------|
| SoFi | 4.50% | $4,500 |
| Marcus | 4.40% | $4,400 |
| Ally | 4.35% | $4,350 |
| Chase | 0.01% | $10 |
| Bank of America | 0.01% | $10 |
| Wells Fargo | 0.01% | $10 |

**Switching from Chase to Marcus at peak = ~$4,390 more per year**

### The Strategic Recommendation

For a wealth management client seeking maximum deposit return:
1. **Online banks (Marcus, Ally, SoFi)** for liquid savings — currently 4%+ APY with no minimum balance requirements and FDIC insurance
2. **CDs** if locking in for 1–2 years — 1-year CDs peaked at 5.0–5.3% in 2023; still 4.5–5.0% at online banks in 2024
3. **Traditional banks** for checking/transaction accounts where rate is less critical — the convenience value is real

The data strongly supports moving idle savings from traditional banks to online banks, with no meaningful downside (both FDIC insured, both digital, online banks often have better UX).
