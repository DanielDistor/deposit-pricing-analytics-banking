# How the Federal Reserve Affects Savings Account Rates

Source: bankrate.com (article on Fed rate impact on savings)
Collected: 2026-05-02

## How It Works

The Federal Reserve sets the federal funds rate — the overnight lending rate banks charge each other. This is the benchmark rate that ripples through the entire financial system:

- When the Fed raises rates: banks can earn more lending money out, and may (or may not) share that with depositors
- When the Fed cuts rates: banks earn less on loans, and typically lower deposit rates too

## The Key Relationship

Savings account APYs are **not legally required** to track the federal funds rate. Banks decide independently how much to pay depositors. This creates the core dynamic in deposit pricing:

- **Online banks** (Marcus, Ally, SoFi, Discover): Compete primarily on rate since they have no physical branches. Must offer attractive APYs to attract deposits. Pass-through tends to be high (60–80%+ of Fed rate changes).
- **Traditional banks** (Chase, BofA, Wells Fargo, Citibank): Compete on convenience, brand, and services. Deposit bases are "sticky" — customers rarely switch banks. Pass-through tends to be very low (<10% of Fed rate changes).

## The 2022–2023 Hiking Cycle

The Fed raised rates 11 times from near 0% (March 2022) to 5.25–5.50% (July 2023) — a 525 basis point increase.

| Bank Type | Starting APY (Mar 2022) | Peak APY (2023) | Pass-Through |
|-----------|------------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Online banks | ~0.50% | 4.50–5.00% | ~75–85% |
| Big-4 banks | ~0.01% | 0.01–0.06% | <5% |

## National Average vs. Best Available

During 2023 peak:
- National average savings APY: ~0.57% (FDIC data)
- Best online savings APY: ~5.00–5.25%
- Fed funds rate: 5.25–5.50%

This 4.68% spread between best-available and national average represents the information/inertia premium that banks capture from depositors who don't switch.

## Implications for Savers

A depositor with $100,000:
- At Chase (0.01% APY): earns ~$10/year
- At Marcus or Ally (4.50% APY): earns ~$4,500/year
- Difference: ~$4,490/year

Over 5 years compounded, the difference exceeds $24,000 on a $100,000 deposit.
